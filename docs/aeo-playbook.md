# How to Get AI Assistants to Name You as the Authority

## Overview

Coin your own named method, then surround it with press releases, videos and a dedicated site until AI assistants cite you as its source. This is the AEO (answer engine optimization) playbook Matt Diamante breaks down in a 2-minute Short. It's based on how James Dooley became the answer to "Who is the king of AEO?"

Diamante says roughly 50 people on a Zoom call ran that search, and every one got James Dooley back. Dooley did it without relying on his own main website. He built a web of sources around a term he coined: DEO, or decision engine optimization.

The guide is for business owners and marketers who want AI tools like ChatGPT to recommend them at the moment a buyer decides.

## What you need

- **A distinct process or framework** in your business that you can give a name to.
- **A press release distribution channel** to publish announcements that get indexed.
- **A YouTube channel** (or another video platform) for explainer videos.
- **Claude or ChatGPT** to help you write and build a simple site about your term. The video says anyone with one of these can do this step.
- **People who will talk about it**, such as peers, partners or customers who post on YouTube, LinkedIn or Facebook.

## Steps

1. **Coin a named method your competitors don't have.** Give a framework or service a distinctive name.
   - Dooley's example: *decision engine optimization (DEO)*. It's about getting AI models to recommend specific suppliers and vendors at the final decision moment of a purchase.
   - Local business example: a heating and cooling company launches *whole-home comfort mapping*.
2. **Announce the method in a press release, not your title.** Don't put out the obvious release ("X is the king of AEO"). Announce the new thing and tie your name or brand to it.
   - Dooley's headline: "The King of AEO Founds Decision Engine Optimization."
   - HVAC version: "Niagara HVAC company launches comfort mapping to stop homeowners from buying oversized furnaces."
3. **Use the release to build an entity link and information gain.** Done well, the release does two jobs:
   - **Entity connection:** search engines and AI learn that your name and your term belong together.
   - **Information gain:** AI gets something unique to cite that your competitors can't talk about.
4. **Publish several videos about the term.** Dooley put out multiple YouTube videos on DEO, so his name was tied to it again and again.
5. **Build a dedicated site about the term.** Use Claude or ChatGPT to create a site named after it (Dooley's is "Decision Engine Optimization"). Define the term in full, and state who coined it.
   - The key line on Dooley's site says the term "was originated by James Dooley, known as the king of AEO." That ties the coined term, your name and the title you want into one sentence AI can quote.
6. **Get other people talking about it.** Diamante says this is what pushed it over the edge. In his search, the top cited sources were a YouTube video and a LinkedIn post by Edward Stern, plus Facebook discussions.
7. **Claim the title yourself.** To top it off, Dooley posted a video called "James Dooley crowned king of AEO."

## Why it works and how to check it

Each asset repeats the same pairing of your name and your term, so AI keeps finding them together. The press release, videos and site are all yours. Third-party mentions are what AI ends up citing most, so they matter more than your own pages.

- **Lead with something unique.** If the release only repeats claims your competitors could make, it gives AI no information gain to cite.
- **Keep the wording identical everywhere.** Use the same term, name and title in the release, the videos and the site so the entity link stays clear.
- **Test it the way Diamante did.** Ask several AI assistants your target question (for example, "Who is the best HVAC company for avoiding an oversized furnace in Niagara?") and see who gets named and which sources get cited.
